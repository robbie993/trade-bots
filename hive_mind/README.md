# The Evolving Hive-Mind Trader

⚠️ **This is a flight simulator, not a trading bot.** Nothing in this package
touches a broker, and the market it trades is *generated by `market.py`*, not
observed. Wire it to a real account and you will lose real money faster than
you can say margin call — and the reason is not that the code is bad. It is
that a system which evolves against its own simulation will get very good at
that simulation and take the habit with it. That failure mode is what most of
this package is built to prevent, and it is worth reading about before running
anything.

```bash
python -m hive_mind                     # watch it think, one day at a time
python -m hive_mind --show-hallucination  # perfect fills vs real ones
python -m hive_mind --lock              # the four-phase pipeline, and its verdict
python -m hive_mind --lock --overfit-demo # the training-data trap, caught live
```

No third-party dependencies, no network, no keys.

It is **not** part of the village. It has no database, no ledger, no firms, no
brokerage and no human gate, and nothing in `src/` imports anything from here.
What it does do is reuse the village's arithmetic rather than growing a second
copy of it:

| Borrowed | Used for |
|---|---|
| `src/money.py` | `D`, `money`, `percent`, `fmt_money` — every cash value is a Decimal, quantised where the ledger quantises |
| `src/trading/indicators.py` | Sharpe, max drawdown, win rate, momentum, stdev — the same statistics the village scores its firms with |
| `src/trading/models.py` | `Bar` (this one subclasses it), `Fill`, `Side`, and the `price()`/`qty()` quantisers |
| `src/trading/execution/paper.py` | `PaperVenue` — what a fill costs in slippage and fees, with impact layered on top |

A second implementation of "what a fill costs" or "how did this do" is a
second answer to those questions, and the first symptom is two parts of one
repository quietly disagreeing about the same run.

## The village

Five parts, each in its own file:

| | |
|---|---|
| **`memory.py`** — the Obsidian brain | Every outcome filed under the regime it happened in (`SPY_VIX35`). Below five trades in a drawer, recall answers "I don't know" rather than averaging two numbers into a conviction. |
| **`scouts.py`** — five fresh minds | Each reads today's bar, consults memory, and proposes one action — then fact-checks itself before speaking. Confidence is earned and lost on its own calls, so the council's weighting means something. |
| **`council.py`** — the debate | Confidence-weighted votes. The winner has to clear the genome's conviction floor, or the answer is `hold`. A 3–2 split among five uncertain scouts is a coin, and trading on it means trading every day and paying the spread for the privilege. |
| **`evolver.py`** — the genetic core | The strategy is eleven numbers. Mutation is seeded, clamped, and **gated by gene** — the parameter that makes phase 4 enforceable rather than aspirational. |
| **`engine.py`** — the book | Marks the position at every close, charges the venue for every share, files what happened. Narrates it all with `verbose=True`. |

And the sixth, which is the point of the exercise:

| | |
|---|---|
| **`lock.py`** — the walk-forward lock | The straitjacket. Four phases a genome must clear before it is allowed near real money, and a gate the engine physically cannot route around. |

## Why "paper profitable, then flip the switch" backfires

The intuition is reasonable: run it in simulation, let it get good, then deploy.
Here is what actually happens, and what this package does about each.

### 1. The simulation hallucination

A naive simulator fills your market order at the last printed close, for free.
The real one crosses the spread, and an order larger than the displayed depth
walks the book away from you.

The bad outcome is not that your backtest is 1% optimistic. It is that **an
evolver scored on free fills learns that size is free**, and evolves toward
more of it. It then arrives live carrying a genome tuned for a market where
slippage does not exist, and the slippage eats exactly the edge it was built
to have. Perfect fills do not merely flatter a backtest; they teach a habit
that only costs money later.

So `Venue` is two layers. The village's `PaperVenue` prices the fill —
slippage that always works against the trader, a fee on the gross — and this
package adds the two terms that model does not have: a half-spread that widens
with VIX, and square-root impact on size. Both are charged on every share in
both directions. There is no size at which trading becomes free and no
direction in which the venue is on your side.

`PerfectVenue` exists only so `--show-hallucination` can print the same genome
scored both ways. Nothing in this package uses it to decide anything:

```
perfect fills: $100,000.00 -> $99,762.46 (-0.24%), sharpe -0.1966, 121 trades
real fills:    $100,000.00 -> $97,840.51 (-2.16%), sharpe -1.9805, 120 trades
```

Two points of return and nearly two points of Sharpe, on one seed, from fills
alone — and the trade count is almost identical, so this is not a different
strategy. It is the same strategy, priced honestly.

### 2. The training-data trap

Evolve against six months of chop and the genome will learn chop perfectly.
Deploy into a rally and it shorts the breakout. It did not get unlucky — it
became a master of the past rather than a survivor of the future, and its
backtest could not tell you that, because its backtest *was* the past it
mastered.

`--lock --overfit-demo` reproduces this deliberately: a trend-follower trained
on a history that is almost all rallies. It passes its holdout — the holdout is
also a rally — and then:

```
[PASSED] phase 2 — the gauntlet
         holdout: +14.40%, sharpe 3.2578, 52 closed trades, win rate 69.23%
         in-sample +64.63% vs holdout +14.40% — a gap of +50.23 points
[FAILED] phase 2b — the stress tests
         chop_2015      2/17 survived, mean  -8.93%
         covid_2020     4/17 survived, mean -13.84%
         survived 36/100 regimes (36%), under the 60% required;
         worst regime (gfc_2008) lost -65.56%
```

A Sharpe of 3.26 on data it had never seen — and it still dies, because a
single holdout that resembles the training data is not evidence of anything.
That is the whole difference between "it passed out-of-sample" and "it
survives markets unlike the one it grew up in", and phase 2b is where the
difference gets measured.

### 3. The echo chamber

A system that learns from its own trades will also learn from its own bugs.
One bad fill caused by a simulator glitch, and it mutates away from a
profitable strategy because it blamed the strategy rather than the data feed.
It evolves a phobia from a software defect, and you find out by missing gains
you never see.

There is no complete defence against this. The partial ones here: memory
refuses to conclude from a small sample; scout confidence is bounded so no
single run can silence a scout for good; every run is seeded, so any result can
be reproduced exactly and inspected rather than argued about.

## The straitjacket

The genome starts locked and opens in four stages. Run `--lock` to watch.

```
Phase 1  HISTORICAL CRUCIBLE   evolve freely — on old data only
Phase 2  THE GAUNTLET          frozen, once, through data it never saw
         THE STRESS TESTS      frozen, across many regimes it never saw
Phase 3  PAPER FORWARD-TEST    frozen, on "current" data, no real money
Phase 4  THE SUICIDE FUND      real money, tiny, sizing genes only
```

**Phase 1 — Historical Crucible.** Split the history 80/20. Evolution runs on
the first part only, and the holdout is *removed from the feed*: training gets
a `WindowFeed` that physically has no bars past the split. Not a cursor, not a
convention — a different object with nothing else in it. A leak from the
holdout into training does not look like a bug, it looks like a strategy that
works, so it has to be impossible rather than discouraged.

Passing phase 1 claims nothing about the strategy. It claims only that training
happened where it was allowed to. The in-sample number it prints is the number
phase 2 exists to distrust.

**Phase 2 — The Gauntlet.** One frozen run through the holdout. Lose money,
draw down past the limit, or make too few trades to say anything, and the
genome is **killed by fingerprint and goes in the graveyard**. That permanence
matters: without it, a failed idea comes back next week with a different seed,
and the pipeline stops being a filter and becomes a machine for finding the one
run in twenty that passed.

**Phase 2b — The Stress Tests.** The same frozen genome across many generated
regimes it never saw — rallies, chop, rate shocks, crashes, rebounds. Requires
a pass rate *and* a worst-case floor, because an average across a hundred runs
hides the one that ends the account.

**Phase 3 — Paper Forward-Test.** Current data, no real money, and evolution
switched off. The engine is deliberately constructed with `online_evolution=True`
here and the phase's permit is empty, so every attempt is refused and counted.
The phase then asserts that zero mutations happened — turning the freeze from a
claim in a docstring into a checked fact about the run that just finished.

**Phase 4 — The Suicide Fund.** $100 of real money, one month, and **only the
sizing genes may move**. `Evolver.mutate` takes the set of genes it is allowed
to touch, and this phase hands it `SIZING_GENES`, so the restriction is in the
mutation function rather than in a comment. Being wrong about size is
recoverable; being wrong about the thesis is not.

Clear it — $100 into $120 with Sharpe above 1.5 — and you may scale up slowly.
On a real run you will find that bar close to unreachable on a small account
over a month once costs are charged, and that is not a bug in the thresholds.
It is the honest shape of the problem.

### The certificate

A cleared genome is licensed by a fingerprint of its **strategy genes only**.
Sizing may drift on real money under that licence; the thesis may not. Change
what the system believes — by mutation, by hand, by import, or by a code path
nobody has written yet — and the fingerprint stops matching and the licence is
void that instant.

```python
lock.permit(genome)
# Permit(allowed=False, reason="phase unproven: the genome is frozen here.
#                               It has proved nothing yet.")
```

The engine asks that question itself, inside `_maybe_evolve`, rather than at
the call site — so no future caller can route around it. With no lock attached
at all, online evolution is simply off: the safe default is the one you get by
forgetting to configure it.

## Where the honesty runs out

Read this part twice.

**The market is generated, not observed.** `market.py` is a seeded random
process with regime parameters. The scenarios named `gfc_2008`, `covid_2020`
and `bear_2022` are *caricatures shaped after* those markets — a crash regime
with a rebound after it — and they are not those markets. A genome that
survives them has survived a caricature. Nothing here has been tested against
a real tape, and a strategy that looks profitable on a random walk has
demonstrated nothing about the world.

**The fills are a model.** Half-spread plus square-root impact is the standard
shape and it is closer to the truth than a free fill at the close, but a real
book has queue position, partial fills, cancels, auctions, halts, and gaps
through your stop. This engine has none of those.

**"Options" are not options.** A scout's `hedge` is a short exposure, not a
put. Real puts have a strike, an expiry, and a volatility surface — none of
which exist here. Pretending otherwise would be the most expensive kind of
wrong, so the vocabulary says `hedge`.

**Out-of-sample bars are still past bars.** A completed pipeline does not mean
the strategy works. It means the one thing that can honestly be checked before
money moves: that the numbers came from data which could not have shaped them.

**Latency does not exist here.** Decisions are made on a close and filled on
that close. A live system makes decisions while the price moves underneath it.

**The genes are Decimal; the tape is not.** Prices, cash, quantities and every
threshold are `Decimal`, because the fingerprint of a genome is what licenses
it and a gene that hashes differently depending on how it was constructed
would make that licence meaningless. The random walk inside `MarketFeed` is
computed in float — it is a random process, not a ledger — and every value
that leaves it has been through `price()` first.

### To point this at reality

Replace `MarketFeed` with something that reads real bars — its whole interface
is `bars()` returning objects with the fields of `Bar`, plus a `window(start,
end)` that returns a feed with fewer of them. Everything downstream, including
the lock, is written against that and nothing else. Then:

- Re-run the pipeline. Expect most genomes to die in phase 2. That is the
  system working, not the system failing.
- Keep `Venue` hostile, and if anything make it more so. Every simulator error
  in your favour becomes a habit the evolver learns.
- Do not lower a threshold because a genome you like keeps failing it. The
  thresholds are the lock. `--lenient` exists to demonstrate the machinery,
  and everything that runs under it has proved nothing.

## Tests

```bash
python -m pytest tests/test_hive_mind.py -q
```

They assert the mechanism, never a verdict — that training cannot reach the
holdout, that a frozen phase is frozen, that real money moves only the sizing
genes, that a killed genome stays killed. Whether a genome makes money on a
generated tape is a fact about the seed, and asserting it would be the same
mistake the lock exists to catch.
